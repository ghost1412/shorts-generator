# Changelog

Release notes for the `shortsflow` package and the Windows installer. Newest first.

## Unreleased

- Without an AI key: shorts are cut to the asked length (they were whole 1.5x context windows, e.g. 45 s for 30 s), and titles and descriptions are clean sentences from the clip (no "uh", no stutters like "it, it", no leading "And", never cut mid-word) instead of the raw first 80 characters of the transcript.
- Highlights video: its title and description are written from what the moments say, with the same grounding check as shorts (it used to send only the pick reasons as "gaming", and a NASA talk got the prompt's example title "Ghost of Tsushima Insane Katana Duel!").
- Highlights video (`--extract_mode long`): moments only seconds apart are joined into one (no stutter crossfade); moments now start and end on sentences (they were joined mid-sentence, and a moment trimmed to fit the length ended wherever the time ran out); the no-LLM fallback no longer overshoots the target by a whole segment; trimmed pieces are never shorter than the length floor (they used to be dropped afterwards, leaving the reel short).
- `.github/workflows/clip_benchmark.yml`: run the clip benchmark on a long public-domain video on GitHub's runners (manual).
- Animated captions on a close-up talking head (large face low in the frame) move to the lower third (y 1400) when the height is left at the default; checked per clip on the finished crop.
- Overlapping clips: settled at sentence boundaries, never trim a moment you picked by time, and a clip that would be a few-second stub is dropped.
- FACTS: a provider outage is no longer retried with new topics (only a disputed topic is).
- README: sample button, Mac/Linux one-liner, timeline strip, clip benchmark.
- Clip edges: a start inside a long sentence goes back to the sentence start (up to 20 s when the clip stays a short), and overlapping clips are split at a sentence instead of mid-sentence. On the sample video: clips that start and end on a sentence went from 2/3 and 1/3 to 3/3.

## 0.7.1 (2026-11-10)

### Fixed
- **Dubbing no longer chops sentences (`--dub_fit smart`, now the default on the command line and in the web app)**: speech recognition can cut a film into 1-2 second lines, and the dub voiced each one alone, so the new voice (2-3 times longer in Hindi) was sped up to its limit and then cut off mid-sentence, with a gap and a lead-in silence around every line. On a real 90-second Hindi test 54 of 59 clips were cut. Smart fit joins short neighbouring lines into sentences, trims the silence off each voice clip, tells the translator how many characters per second the language's voice speaks (Hindi, Marathi, Arabic and Cyrillic languages are slower than Latin ones), lets a voice that runs long continue into the next gap (up to about 3 s behind the picture) instead of cutting it, and puts the subtitles on the voice. The same test: 25 sentences, 2 clips shortened. `--dub_fit classic` keeps the old behaviour; library callers of `dub_video` still default to classic. A dub made before this change has different line boundaries, so it is translated again.
- **The online voice service sometimes returned nothing for a line** ("No audio was received") when several lines were requested together; those lines are now asked again (a few times) instead of leaving a silent gap.
- **Newer FFmpeg builds (8.x) reject `-filter_complex_script`**, which the dub mix, the highlights-reel captions and the transcript editor used; ShortsFlow now detects which spelling the installed FFmpeg understands.
- A dub with hundreds of lines no longer crashes on Windows (the mix command was longer than Windows allows); a stopped dub can be continued, reusing its saved translation and voices (new "Continue an earlier dub" choice in the web app).
- QUOTE rejects lines about harming yourself or others, death or weapons; HORROR shows its own title and dark footage instead of a random category and clip; REDDIT captions no longer print two words on top of each other.

### New
- **Step-by-step videos from a text file (`--mode TRIP --steps_file steps.txt`)**: a recipe, a DIY job, a workout or a tutorial. The file has `Title:`, `Hook:`, `Needs:` (or `Ingredients:`, `Materials:`, `Tools:`), `End:` and one `1. Step title | caption` per step; the clips go in step order (one folder per step, or one folder in filming order). It fills in the titles, captions, the needs card and the closing line and switches on the defaults that suit such a video (cinematic look, vertical, result first, fast pace, text clear of the phone app's bars); options you pass yourself win. A different number of folders and steps gets a plain warning. A new recap kind `steps` (`--recap_kind steps`) supplies the wording. In the web app, the Recap tab has a "Step by step" kind and a box for the steps (same format as the file).
- **Faster, more readable recaps for step-by-step videos (a recipe, a build, a tutorial)**: `--recap_opening result` opens with one shot of the finished result and goes straight into the first part (no other glimpses, no title shot); `--recap_pace fast` shortens the opening and the ending and lets a part be as short as about 3 s (the normal pace keeps at least 6 s per part, so 7 parts take about 55 s whatever `--recap_length` says), shows the title and caption straight away and keeps them on screen for the whole shot, drops the giant stacked-word titles and puts the caption on a dark panel so it reads on any picture; `--recap_speed N` plays a longer stretch of each step shot N times faster (a time-lapse for kneading, frying, stirring; the clip's own sound is dropped on those shots, use `--music`); `--recap_end result|none` finishes on the result shot with a line (`--recap_end_text`) or on the last shot instead of the closing card; `--recap_counter` adds a small "PART 3 / 7" label; `--recap_safe_text` keeps titles and captions in the upper-middle band, clear of the top bar and the buttons and caption that Reels, Shorts and TikTok draw at the bottom; `--recap_ingredients "250g flour|1 cup water|..."` adds a card that lists what is needed (amounts in gold), at the end by default or before the first part with `--recap_ingredients_at start`. All off by default. `--trip_stops` now also accepts `|` between names, like `--recap_lines`.
- **Dub options**: translation tone (`--dub_tone`: funny, sarcastic, dramatic, formal, casual), voice delivery (`--dub_delivery`: excited, calm, serious: speed and pitch only), subtitle size and position (`--dub_caption_size`, `--dub_caption_position above` clears subtitles already burned into the video), and an AI text provider choice on the Start here, Dub and Recap tabs.
- A demo page for GitHub Pages (`index.html`) and a Mac/Linux/Docker route spelled out next to the Windows installer.

## 0.7.0 (2026-11-10)

### New
- **Game framing that chooses for you (`--game_framing auto|fit|crop`, "Framing for game footage" in the Start-here and Clip tabs)**: `fit` keeps the whole picture over a blurred copy (the minimap, HUD and the game's own subtitles stay), `crop` is a full-screen portrait crop, and `auto` (the default) looks at frames inside the chosen moments and picks `fit` when a HUD or subtitles sit at the sides and `crop` when the picture is clean. It is a heuristic tuned on a handful of games; the log says what it chose and why, and the dropdown or flag overrides it. `--game_crop` still works and means `crop`.
- **Captions off (`--caption_position off`, "No captions" in the caption-position dropdowns)**: draws none of our captions, for videos whose own subtitles are already burned in. A manual choice, never guessed; it is remembered when a clip is re-rendered.
- **Second-opinion fact check for FACTS and TRIVIA**: after a script is written, one more model call judges every claim on its own. A script is kept only when the judge agrees the true facts are true and the lie is false; otherwise a new one is written (up to 4 tries). If the judge cannot be reached the script is kept with a warning, and `SHORTSFLOW_FACT_CHECK=off` turns it off. It is a model checking a model: it lowers wrong facts, it does not remove them.
- **Recap text and result-first opening in the web Recap tab** ("A line for each part", "Open with the result first"), plus the earlier command-line options below.
- A short "Liked the result? A star helps..." line after a finished run in a terminal (not in logs or the web app; `SHORTSFLOW_NO_STAR_HINT=1` turns it off).

### Fixed
- **Gemini 3 cut replies off**: its hidden thinking tokens count against the output limit, so short JSON replies (FACTS asked for 1024) were truncated and every attempt failed. The limit now leaves room for the thinking.
- **WYR no longer asks the model for made-up vote percentages** (nothing showed them).
- **Game mode remembers the moments it picked** (`highlights_game.json`, reused with `--use_cache` when the settings match).
- **REDDIT and QUOTE** failed when `--session_dir` was a folder that did not exist yet; it is created now.
- Cut sounds follow the dissolve overlap when `--auto_edit` is combined with `--transitions dissolve`.

### Earlier in this release
- **Recap text for each part and an opening that shows the result (`--recap_lines`, `--recap_open_result`)**: `--recap_lines "Fry the nuts in ghee | Roast the khoya"` gives each part (a recipe step, a stop) one line of text, shown under the part's title and on its shots; long lines are drawn larger and white on a soft dark panel so they stay readable over busy footage. `--recap_open_result` makes the opening shots come from the LAST part (the finished dish, the summit, the first dance) before the story starts. With the cinematic style the hook is shown once on the first opening shot instead of being stamped on every one.
- **Dissolve transition (`--transitions dissolve`)**: every shot melts into the next over 0.6 s (xfade and acrossfade), with no zoom and no dip to black per shot; the recap gets shorter by 0.6 s per join. It is a choice you make (the Auto-edit styles still use the zoom punch or the mix) and it re-encodes the final join once. Shots with a stale rotate tag on an upright picture are joined as they are shown.
- **Game footage mode (`--content game`, "This is game footage" in the Start-here tab)**: the normal picker builds its candidates only from stretches where someone speaks, so a silent boss fight or a gameplay highlight could never be chosen. Game mode measures every 30-second stretch of the video (words per second, sound peaks and variation, picture motion), labels each one story (cutscene, dialogue), action (fight, gameplay, even when Whisper hears words in the noise) or quiet, scores each by its own kind and mixes them (`--game_mix`, default half story and half action; a dropdown in the web form). The first 3% of the video (title montage) is skipped, and the clips use the `fit` layout (the whole picture over a blurred copy, so the minimap, the HUD and the game's own subtitles are not cut off); `--game_crop` keeps the centre crop. Tuned on one cutscene-and-boss-fight video; real gameplay may need different numbers. Clip titles for action moments are generic ("Action moment") when no AI text provider answers.
- **Caption position and size (`--caption_position top|middle|bottom`, `--caption_size small|large|xlarge`, dropdowns in the Start-here tab)**: the animated captions can sit at the top, middle or bottom and be 0.8x to 1.6x their normal size; the top edge stays where it is when they grow. Normal height is still the default (and below the picture with `--layout fit`); an explicit `--subtitle_y_pos` wins. The size is remembered when a single clip is re-rendered. Needs `--use_remotion`.

## 0.6.0 (2026-11-08)

### Fixed
- **NEWS never posts a placeholder, and stays on its category**: with no fresh headline the run stops instead of publishing a made-up story. Top stories are only used for the world category, sensitive topics (politics, geopolitics, health scares, deaths) and non-news pages (lists, quizzes, reviews, deals) are skipped, each cat persona keeps its own voice, and credits show the outlet name instead of a raw link.
- **Puzzle formats stop repeating**: hooks, characters, find-it targets, emoji answers and odd-one-out effects avoid what was used recently (`engine/puzzle_variety.py`). Emoji Guess rejects overused answers (Lion King, Titanic and similar) and its voiceover matches the on-screen 5-second countdown.
- **Odd One Out is harder and fairer**: a random difficulty (easy, medium, hard), a bigger grid, a generic hint when it is not easy, a shrinking timer bar, and no answer reveal at the end.
- **CHAT_STORY, HORROR, REDDIT and QUOTE no longer repeat one story**: each picks a fresh premise instead of copying the prompt's example or dropping the category word into a template. HORROR strips stage directions like `<Dramatic pause>` before the voice reads them, REDDIT can no longer read raw JSON aloud when a reply fails to parse (the JSON parser also reads single-quoted dicts now), and QUOTE is always credited to "Unknown" so a made-up line never carries a real person's name. A failed generation stops the run instead of publishing a placeholder.
- **EXPLAINER asks for the animation code as a code block** instead of inside a JSON string (small models got the escaping wrong and every try failed to parse), rejects placeholder titles, and writes a new script when a render crashes (up to 3 tries). Videos from a small local model are still plain text on black.
- **GUESS_SOUND answers only with a sound it has**: the mode used to play synthesized rain for any answer outside a six-sound list (two of the links were dead). It now draws from a bundled pack of real recordings (`assets/sounds`, from Wikimedia Commons; `manifest.json` lists each licence) and adds a credit line to the description for CC BY-SA clips. The pack has 7 clips so far, so the mode repeats quickly and is not part of the scheduled mix.
- **Recap**: place-name fallbacks hide names in non-Latin scripts, and the Recap tab keeps its extra options in one collapsed section.

### Docs
- Recap demo GIF built from CC0 stock clips (with a note on how it was made) and Hugging Face Space files in `space/`.

### New
- **Scheduled runs now mix formats**: the daily workflow used to be pinned to FACTS ("Spot the Lie"), so the channel only got that format. Each cron run now picks a mode by weighted chance (Spot the Lie, This or That, Emoji Guess, Odd One Out, Find It, Trivia, Riddle, Top 5, Story). Set the repo variable `SCHEDULED_MODE` to one mode name for the old behaviour, or to your own weights such as `WYR:30,FACTS:10`.
- **Recap transitions instead of plain cuts (`--transitions mix|punch|whip|flash|glitch`, "Transitions between shots" on the Recap tab)**: a fast zoom punch with a short blur, a whip pan with horizontal motion blur, a white flash, an RGB-split glitch, or a mix that changes on every cut. The effect is built from the end of one shot and the start of the next, so the shots are still joined without re-encoding; the hook text stays put. How long it lasts follows `--edit_strength` (subtle 0.14 s, medium 0.2 s, hyped 0.26 s). Runs on this PC only.
- **Subject cut-out transition (`--transitions subject`, "Subject cut-out" in the Recap tab)**: the person in the shot stays in front, grows a little and fades out while the background wipes to the next shot. A small segmentation model (torchvision LRASPP MobileNetV3, about 13 MB, downloaded once by torch) finds the person on this PC, on the CPU. A cut without a clear person (none, too small, too big, walking out of frame) gets one of the other transitions instead. Needs `pip install shortsflow[subject]` (`torch`, `torchvision`, `opencv-python`); without them every cut uses the other types. Each cut-out overlaps the end of one shot and the start of the next, so it makes the edit 0.5 s shorter. The person is looked for only in the sharp picture of a letterboxed shot (not in its blurred bars), and the shot's own titles and captions are filled in under the person so they do not ghost over the next shot. **Outline looks** (`--subject_look auto|plain|glow|glow_sweep|spotlight`, `--subject_color auto|white|gold|cyan|pink|#rrggbb`, two dropdowns in the Recap tab): before the background changes, a bright outline builds up round the person (optionally with a light sweep and a darker background). The outline is experimental, OFF by default and hidden in the app (command line only: `--subject_look glow`). The cut-out is only used where ONE person is in the shot (not groups or crowds) and at most twice per recap, spaced apart (`--subject_max`, default 2); the other cuts use the other transitions. The bridge is up to 0.3 s longer for the build-up; the edit still gets 0.5 s shorter per bridge.
- **Transitions follow the edit style by default (`--transitions auto`)**: `--auto_edit hype` uses the mix, `vlog` the zoom punch, `podcast` and `educational` plain cuts; an explicit choice (including `none`) always wins. The compiled highlight reel (`--extract_mode long`) uses the same choice for the joins between moments (zoom punch = zoom in, whip = slide, flash = fade through white, glitch = pixelate).

## 0.5.0 (2026-11-07)

### New
- **Recap: fewer look-alike, dark and junk shots**: each shot is now compared with the two shots before it (also across days) by its picture as well as its description, so two views of the same bay no longer follow each other; very dark shots, ground close-ups, animals, buffet tables and other empty or accidental shots are swapped for a better spare, or left out when the day has other shots. The most striking shot of the last day goes last. Works with or without picture descriptions.
- Recap of weddings and events: each folder you give becomes one part, in the order given (named after the folder unless you name the parts), and parts with far more videos than shots are sampled (`--recap_sample auto|all|N`, a choice in the web form) so hundreds of videos are not all read end to end.
- Recap gets the Start-here editing options: the Auto-edit director (`--auto_edit` + `--edit_strength`: a slow push-in or pull-out on every shot, sound effects at the cuts, the style's colour look), a colour filter (`--video_filter`; Automatic gives a look only when the footage suits it, otherwise none) and `--clean_audio` for the clips' own sound. The web recap length is no longer capped at 5 minutes (up to 60; the engine has no limit).
- Recap is no longer only for trips: the tab (now "Recap (trip, wedding, event)") has a "What kind of videos are these?" choice - trip, wedding, event or party, family memories (`--recap_kind`). The wording, the section names (Part 1, Part 2, or your own part names via `--trip_stops`) and the options change with it; non-trip recaps never look up places, and `--recap_keep_sound` keeps the clips' own sound (vows, speeches) under the music.
- Start here: a "Colour filter" dropdown (12 looks, "Let the AI choose", or none). Left on Automatic, the Auto-edit style still picks its own look.
- Review card: a "Caption height" slider in "Edit this short" moves the animated captions up or down and re-renders that one short (`python -m engine.rerender RUN INDEX --caption_y 900`); the height is remembered for later edits and undo restores it.
- **Trip recap: place names on the screen, fewer wrong names, new clips on request**: (1) the recap used to show vague picture captions ("Camera moving along a dirt road") and kept the place names only in the itinerary. The cinematic look now shows the place name on the shot where the place changes (and the day titles are the real places), the other looks show the place on every shot; administrative areas ("Westford District") are never shown. (2) Names are more careful: the AI is told to answer blank when it is not sure, shots from a plane are "In flight", and one name given to two places more than 2.5 km apart (two different lakes both called "Blue Lake") triggers a corrective request and then only the first keeps it. (3) `places_review.md` is written next to every recap: each place with its GPS, a Google Maps link and the name used, and `--place_names "42.985,78.465=North Lake; 42.991,78.326=South Lake"` sets a name yourself (it is remembered). (4) `--recap_avoid <earlier recap folder or itinerary.json>` picks NEW moments, skipping the ones earlier recaps used; without it the same footage always gives the same picks.
- **Animated captions render about 3x faster on PCs with an NVIDIA card**: the caption renderer (Remotion) used a fixed 2 parallel workers there, so most CPU cores sat idle. It now uses half the CPU threads (at least 2, at most 8, and no more than one per 2 GB of RAM). Measured on a 16-thread PC with an RTX 4060: 2 workers 6.8 frames/s, 6 workers 18.8, 10 workers 22.8; a 30-second short went from 153 s to 53 s end to end, and the output was checked to decode cleanly. To keep the PC free while you game, set `SHORTSFLOW_REMOTION_CONCURRENCY=2` (a number like 4 or a percentage like 50% also works).
- **Clip quality report for finished runs (`python scripts/clip_benchmark.py sessions/run_a sessions/run_b`)**: measures what can be checked without a human or an LLM: how many clips are inside the asked length, start and end where a sentence starts and ends, overlap between clips, how much of the video the clips are spread over, words spoken per second (dead air) and how many titles and descriptions say nothing the clip does not. With `--gold gold.json` (moments you chose by hand) it also reports recall and precision. Run it before and after a change to see whether the picks got better or worse. The check on titles ignores capitals that are only style (Title Case, sentence openers, "Follow", "I've") and checks a description only for invented figures.
- **Auto-enhance for shorts and the trip recap (`--enhance auto`, `--enhance_strength subtle|medium`; tick-boxes on Start here and the Trip recap tab)**: fixes each video (or, for shorts, each clip's own stretch of the source) the way an editor does before styling it. A few frames of every video are measured (brightness range, colour cast, colourfulness, noise) and only what is off is corrected, within safe limits: dark clips are lifted and very bright ones toned down (gamma), a really flat picture gets its black and white points stretched, a clear colour cast is pulled back part of the way (a vivid warm sunset or blue lake is left alone, that is the look), a dull clip gets a little colour, a light sharpening is added, and dark noisy clips get light denoising. The correction runs before the colour look. Everything is measured and rendered locally with FFmpeg. It cannot add detail that is not in the footage. In a recap the shots are corrected towards the typical brightness of the whole recap, so neighbouring shots match instead of jumping between dark and bright. For shorts, what was fixed is shown on the clip's review card ("Picture fixed: too dark, lifted"). Off by default. On 173 phone clips of one trip about 45% needed a correction, mostly a colour cast or a dark shot.
- **Reuse an earlier run from the browser app (Start here and Trip recap)**: the new "Earlier runs: open, continue or reuse" section (one place instead of three separate boxes: open a finished run, continue one that stopped, run again reusing its work) lists earlier runs, finished or not. Pick one, change any setting (captions, brand, extras, music...) and start: the new run is seeded with the earlier run's transcript, picture descriptions, chosen moments, crop and silence analysis and the pieces already cut, so only the final rendering is done again, and the video does not have to be added again. A selector chooses what to skip: "Skip everything already done" (transcript, moments, cut pieces) or "Skip transcription only" (pick the moments again; needed when you change the clip count, length or layout). Finished clips are never reused, so a changed look is always applied. The Trip recap tab has the same box (picture descriptions and place names are reused, and the earlier recap's videos when you add none), plus three new options that were CLI-only: "Look at the pictures too" (`--visual_context`), "Make a portrait AND a landscape version" (`--recap_versions both`; both files are in the download box) and "Name the places from the videos' GPS" (on by default, with a note that the rounded position goes to OpenStreetMap; untick = `--recap_no_geocode`). Before this only a run that was stopped before it finished could be continued.
- **Upload finished videos to a Google Drive folder** (`python -m engine.drive_upload FILE... --folder <folder link>`, and `--upload_drive <folder link>` with `--mode TRIP`): resumable uploads into a folder you give, one file after another, printing each file's link. One-time set-up: a Google Cloud OAuth client of type Desktop app with the Drive API enabled, saved as `client_secrets_drive.json`; the first upload opens the browser to sign in and saves `token_drive.json` (both git-ignored). It asks for full Drive access because Google's narrower `drive.file` scope cannot write into a folder you created yourself. Nothing is uploaded unless you ask. The browser app has an optional "Upload to Google Drive" folder box on Start here and on the Trip recap tab: when the run finishes, the finished videos are uploaded and the status says so.
- **Trip recap: the trip title no longer repeats Day 1's first shot**: the cinematic trip title was drawn over the same footage that Day 1 then opened with, so the same clip played twice in a row. The title now takes a shot of its own.
- **Trip recap polish**: `--recap_rotate "183047=270"` forces the turn of a clip that came out sideways (a part of its file name and clockwise degrees; 0 cancels a wrong automatic turn). Shots that the picture description calls empty, blurry or accidental ("an empty bench", "blurry footage of a pocket") are swapped for a better one like repeats are. The cinematic look no longer reserves time for title cards it does not have, so the recap gets closer to the requested length.
- **Trip recap: places from the videos' GPS, no look-alike shots, sideways clips turned upright, a closing card**: phones write the filming position into every video, so the recap now groups each day's videos into places (more than 3 km apart = a new place), names them (the free OpenStreetMap service for the map's name, then the AI only when the coordinates, the map and the pictures agree on a place it knows; never invented) and uses the names in the itinerary, captions, the trip title line and a new closing card. The coordinates (rounded to ~100 m) are sent to OpenStreetMap: `--recap_no_geocode` or `SHORTSFLOW_GEOCODE=off` keeps everything on the PC. Shots of the same scene (the same spot filmed within 4 minutes, or two neighbours with nearly the same picture description) are no longer used twice: the nearest different shot takes the place. With `--visual_context`, Gemini also checks two frames of each used video (it says on which side of the picture the top of the scene is) and the recap turns a clip that is stored sideways although its file says otherwise upright, only when both frames agree. Place names are saved with the run (`place_names.json`) so a re-run gives the same names. Places are named as a tourist would call them, not by administrative area: the map is searched for named viewpoints, waterfalls, lakes, attractions and parks within about 3 km of the GPS position, and those are offered to the AI, which picks the attraction (translated to English), or, if none is known, describes what the pictures show ("Pine forest trail") instead of answering "Westford District". The recap ends on a short closing card with the title and the places instead of fading out on the last shot.
- **Cinematic travel film for the trip recap (`--recap_style cinematic`, and now the automatic choice for widescreen footage)**: modelled on travel films (2.35:1 widescreen, soft warm or cool grade, slow fades, almost no text). There are no black title cards: the trip title and each day's title appear over the footage, one layer after another (small credit, wide-spaced title, rule and line). Each title looks at a frame of its shot to decide where to sit (the calmest part of the picture), whether to use light or dark text, and which accent colour to use, and the layout rotates (centred, left-aligned, stacked words). The grade (warm for sand, rock and sunsets; cool for water, ice and forest) and the frame shape come from the footage too: `--recap_style auto` (the new default) uses cinematic 2.35:1 for mostly widescreen videos and the existing hype 9:16 for vertical phone clips. With `--music`, moment lengths are rounded to whole beats so the cuts land on the music. `--recap_aspect 9:16|16:9|2.35:1` sets the frame shape. See `docs/TEMPLATES.md`.
- **Trip recap from a Google Photos shared album link** (`--mode TRIP --trip_album https://photos.app.goo.gl/... [--trip_day 2026-10-16]`): the album's videos (not longer than 5 minutes; a 20-minute recording is skipped) are downloaded into one folder per day, named so they sort in filming order, and then made into the recap. Google's own Photos API no longer lets apps read a user's library (since March 2025), so this reads the public shared-album page the way a browser does. It is unofficial: if Google changes the page the command says so and you can download the videos yourself and use `--trip_videos`. Only albums shared by link work, nothing is uploaded, and no login is needed. Downloads are resumable (files already there are kept).
- **Trip recap (`--mode TRIP`, browser tab "Trip recap")**: give several videos in filming order (or one folder per day, or files named day1, day2) and get one vertical recap that opens like a trailer: a hook line ("Worth the early start") over fast glimpses of the most beautiful shots (colour and contrast decide, or the vision model's description with `--visual_context`), then the trip title and the whole trip with a title card for each day or stop. Each day gets a few lively moments (the loudest stretches, spread over the whole day) with a short caption, and an itinerary (`itinerary.md` and `.json`) is written next to the video. For a one-day tour name the stops: `--trip_stops "North Lake, South Lake, Red Canyon"`. The AI writes only from your note (`--user_context`), the stop names and the picture descriptions; with nothing to go on a caption stays empty rather than invented. Example: `python main.py --mode TRIP --trip_videos clip1.mp4 clip2.mp4 clip3.mp4 --trip_name "Mountain tour: two lakes and a canyon" --trip_stops "North Lake, South Lake, Red Canyon" --user_context "We booked a 17 hour tour and left at 5 am" --recap_hook "Worth the early start" --recap_length 60`. Options: `--recap_style plain` (no opening), `--music file`, `--layout fit|crop`.
- **Dubbing can keep the music and remove only the original voice** (`--dub_original music`, now the default; also the first choice in the Dub tab). The video's sound is split into voice and everything else with `demucs` (`pip install demucs`; about a minute per minute of video on the CPU, cached per session), and only the voice-free part stays under the new voice. Before, the original language was always faintly audible unless all sound was removed. Without `demucs` it falls back to the old faint-original behaviour and says so. The translation step now also tells the model the lines come from speech recognition (so a misheard name is translated as the likely name) and uses `--user_context` ("God of War 3: Kratos, Zeus ...") to fix names and terms.
- **Explainer videos from fixed scenes** (`python -m engine.explainer_template beats.json out_dir`): each beat has a sentence to say and a ready-made Manim visual (title, counter of dots for "1 in 10 billion", sigma bar, zoom-in rings, bullet list). The narration is voiced first and every visual lasts exactly as long as its sentence, so nothing freezes or goes silent. The AI-written `--mode EXPLAINER` scenes could stop animating after a few seconds and leave empty boxes; the same mode also left the file longer than the voice (a silent tail), which is fixed: it now ends with the voice.
- **Long videos are searched one stretch at a time**: for videos over 20 minutes the AI used to get the whole transcript in one request. Small models lose the timeline that way: on a 64-minute recording it returned only 6 candidates, three of them with invented times (like 16596 s in a 3842 s video), and never looked at the dialogue scene at 61:40. Now the transcript is cut into stretches of about 10 minutes (at most 8), each stretch is asked for its own moments, answers outside their stretch are ignored, and everything is ranked together afterwards. Videos up to 20 minutes behave as before.
- **Picture descriptions can use Gemini** (default when `GEMINI_API_KEY` is set): the few small frames per moment are sent to Google, so nothing runs on the PC's GPU, and the descriptions are noticeably better than a local 12B model's (it names characters and reads subtitles). The log says which one is used. `SHORTSFLOW_VISION=ollama` keeps everything local (nothing leaves the PC); `=gemini` forces Gemini. Used by `--visual_context` and the trip recap.
- **Story-aware picking (`--visual_context`, plus `--user_context "what the video is about"`)**: the AI used to judge clips only from what is SAID, so a video where the speaker chats to the viewers while the story plays on screen (a game, a film recap) lost its best scenes. With `--visual_context` a local vision model (Ollama: gemma3, qwen2.5vl, llava, llama3.2-vision, minicpm-v or moondream; `SHORTSFLOW_VISION_MODEL` picks one) looks at 3 frames of each candidate and writes what is on screen, including readable subtitles. The ranking step then sees it next to the words ("ON SCREEN: ...") and so does the title writer. Your `--user_context` text now also reaches the ranking step. Descriptions are cached in `visual_context.json` in the session folder. It is slower (roughly 5-20 s per frame) and is skipped with a note (`ollama pull gemma3:12b`) when no vision model is installed. In the browser app: "What is this video about?" and "Look at the picture too" under "Pick the moments".
- **Exact moments (`--moments "7:00-7:45, 1:01:40-1:02:30"`)**: make shorts from the exact times you give instead of letting the AI choose. The AI picks what is said, so a silent cutscene or a scene you already know can be missing from its choices. Times can be `7:00-7:45`, `1:01:40-1:02:30` or plain seconds, one per line or comma separated; a lone time (`7:00`) starts a short of the target length there. Your times are cut exactly as given (no sentence snapping) and then get the usual captions, auto-edit and effects. A typo is reported before the long analysis starts. In the browser app: "Or give exact times" under "Pick the moments".
- **Length of each short on the Start here tab** (15 to 60 seconds, default 30). It was fixed at the engine default before. Clips still end at a sentence end, so they can be a few seconds longer or shorter than the aim.
- **Stop button in the browser app** (Start here, Clip, Dub, Photo reel and Generate tabs): ends the running pipeline together with everything it started (FFmpeg, Node, the browser behind the animated captions) and says what was kept. Previously a run could only be ended by closing programs by hand.
- **Continue an unfinished run** (Start here): a run that was stopped, closed or crashed can carry on from where it stopped. Each Start here run now saves its exact command; "Continue an unfinished run" reruns it in the same folder with the cache on, so the transcript, the chosen moments and every segment already cut are reused. Cut segments are written under a temporary name and only renamed once FFmpeg finished, so a stopped run never leaves a truncated segment that a continue would trust.
- **Dubbing: control what happens to the video's own sound (`--dub_original quiet|duck|mute`)**: the original voice used to stay at full volume everywhere the new voice was not speaking, so a dub still sounded mostly like the original. Now the default `quiet` keeps it as a faint bed (about -18 dB, lowered further under the new voice), `duck` keeps the old behaviour, and `mute` removes the original sound completely. The Dub tab has a "The video's own sound" choice. There is no way to remove only the voice without separating the sounds, so the original voice stays faintly audible unless you choose `mute`.
- **Dubbing warns when little speech was recognised**: if speech recognition finds speech in less than 40% of the video, the log and the Dub tab say so and suggest the spoken language and a bigger model (`SHORTSFLOW_WHISPER_MODEL=large-v3`), instead of quietly leaving the rest in the original language.
- **Thumbs up / down on clips**: every review card has "👍 Good pick" and "👎 Not good". Ratings are saved only on your computer. After at least 8 ratings with both thumbs, the picker learns which clip length you tend to like and nudges candidate scores by at most 5 points toward it (a tie-breaker, never an override). `SHORTSFLOW_NO_FEEDBACK=1` switches it off.
- **`--layout fit`**: shows the WHOLE picture centred at full width over a blurred, darkened, zoomed copy of itself, instead of cropping the sides off a landscape video. Made for screen recordings and gameplay where a centre crop loses the action. Animated captions move below the picture automatically (an explicit `--subtitle_y_pos` is kept). Available in the CLI, API, MCP and the browser app's Framing choice.
- **Number pop-ups (`--stat_pops`)**: when someone says a striking number ("$200", "73 percent", "3 million", "in 10 years"), it appears big on screen for 1.7 s the moment it is said. Years, small numbers and numbers in the first or last second are skipped, and numbers are kept at least 4 s apart. Needs `--use_remotion`; on by default in the podcast, hype and educational auto-edit styles (with a soft pop sound). Works with re-render.
- **Where did the time go?** The pipeline now prints `[Time] phase: +Ns (total Ns)` after each phase (outline and audio, motion analysis, LLM picks, ranking, face tracking, rendering, titles), so a slow run can be diagnosed from its log.
- **`--language`, auto-edit and more in the REST API, MCP and browser app**: `/v1/clips` and MCP `extract_clips` accept the spoken language and the auto-edit options (the API also `caption_lang`, `cold_open`, `clean_audio`); the Clip tab has a spoken-language box and the auto-edit controls. Shorts are capped at 60 s everywhere: the API (`target_duration` up to 60) and the Clip tab slider no longer promise more. The README lists the defaults of each surface.
- **Auto-edit director (`--auto_edit podcast|hype|educational|vlog`, `--edit_strength subtle|medium|hyped`)**: one choice edits a clip the way an editor would. It turns on the right mix of pause and um cuts, audio clean-up, animated captions in a fitting style, hook title, punch-in zooms (strength-scaled), stock cutaways, a teaser opening, a colour grade, and a new **sound-effects stage**: soft whooshes and pops mixed under zooms, cutaways and the teaser cut (synthesised, no downloads). Your own flags always win. It writes `edit_plan.json` (what was chosen and what each clip actually got, including the longest stretch with no visual change) and shows a one-line "Edited: ..." summary on each review card. Works with re-render. In the browser app: "Auto-edit style" and "How much editing" on the Start here tab. The director also fills any stretch longer than 7 s with no zoom by adding a gentle push-in in the middle of it, so a long calm patch never looks frozen (sound effects are only used for the quick punch-ins).
- **Photo reels with a type**: `--mode PHOTO_REEL --reel_type memories|travel|product|then_now|countdown|tribute` (and a new Photo reel tab in the browser app). The type decides the order (photo dates for memories/travel), how long each photo stays (first and last held longer), the captions (Day 1 / Day 2, #5 ... #1, THEN / NOW, your own lines), the title, the closing line and the look (flash, motion, colour grade). Blurry and near-duplicate photos are skipped automatically. `--prompt` is the title or name, `--reel_text` adds your own words per photo; without `--music` a built-in track plays. A reel without `--reel_type` renders exactly as before.

### Fixed
- A rate-limited Gemini can no longer freeze a recap: the picture-rotation check waits about a minute at most, then uses the check on this PC and stays off Gemini for five minutes.
- Recaps read file names from itineraries written on any OS (a Windows itinerary used to break the "avoid earlier moments" check on Linux, which also failed the daily run).
- **Trip recap: a clip turned sideways in the middle of a video, and the same place named twice**: the sideways check judged a whole video from its start, so a phone that was turned partway through (only the later stretch is sideways) was never corrected. Every moment is now judged on its own, from two frames inside it, by Gemini or, when Gemini is rate-limited, by a local check that looks for the sky on the edges of the picture (strict: it stays silent when unsure). The same spot filmed on different days is named once from all its pictures and keeps one name; when the AI is unsure the map's own name is used instead of leaving the stop unnamed; "In flight" is no longer listed as a place on the title and closing card; a day's title can show up to three places. With `--recap_versions both` the landscape version reuses the portrait version's place names, so the same spot has the same name in both.
- **Picture descriptions of different videos were mixed up in the trip recap**: the saved descriptions (visual_context.json) were keyed only by the moment's start and end time, and many clips share the same window (0.0-4.8 s), so one video's description was given to another. That produced wrong captions, wrong place names and wrong sideways-clip checks. The key now includes the video's name. Old visual_context.json files hold mixed-up text and should not be reused (a recap started from one re-describes the moments).
- **A clip's title and description no longer say what the clip does not**: only the alternative titles were checked against the transcript before. Now the main title and the description's sentences are checked too: a number or name that the clip, the hook and your own note do not say triggers ONE rewrite request that names what to remove, and if the rewrite still invents something the title falls back to the hook or the first sentence said, and an invented description line is dropped (hashtags are kept).
- **A failing daily channel is no longer silent**: a small watchdog workflow (`daily_watchdog.yml`, every 6 hours) opens one issue labelled `daily-failing` when the last two scheduled runs of the daily job failed, and closes it by itself when a run succeeds again. It only reads workflow runs and never posts anything. The decision logic is in `scripts/daily_watchdog.py` and is covered by tests.
- **The daily channel's renders crashed on the Linux runner when `--bitrate` was given**: since `--bitrate` was passed on to the animated-caption (Remotion) renderer as `--video-bitrate`, Remotion's encoder exited a few frames into the render (`write EPIPE`) and every scheduled run since 4 Nov failed (the daily job always passes `--bitrate 12000k`). Remotion output is now set by `--quality` (CRF) only, as before; `--bitrate` still applies to the FFmpeg stages. The E2E workflow can now be started by hand with extra flags (for example `--bitrate 12000k`) to test exactly what the daily job runs.
- **Dubbing no longer pretends a failed voice worked, and names the usual cause**: when Microsoft's voice service rejects an old `edge-tts` (error 403), the empty voice files are discarded and the error now says to run `pip install -U edge-tts` instead of blaming your internet.
- **Post-processing no longer skips a clip when Windows briefly locks the new file** ("[WinError 5] Access is denied" while swapping in the cleaned-audio, sound-effect, teaser, cutaway or branded version): the swap now waits and retries for a few seconds, and a failed audio clean-up no longer leaves a temp copy behind.
- **A render could look hung after the video was written** (animated captions off): the cleanup compared the clip's audio frame by frame (`!=` on MoviePy clips), which took minutes for a sped-up clip and a few seconds for every other clip. It now compares by identity (about 9 s saved per clip).
- **`--tighten_mode speed` no longer drifts the captions** in the older renderer: the caption words are re-timed from the same plan that sped up the silences, so they stay with the speech.

## 0.4.1 (2026-11-04)

### New
- **Use the browser app from a phone on another network**: `shortsflow webui --share` creates a temporary public link (Gradio tunnel). Shared links and apps listening on the network now always ask for a login: a password is generated and printed at start, or choose your own with `--auth user:password`.
- **Cold open (`--cold_open`)**: each short can start with its strongest later sentence as a 1.5-6 s teaser, then play from the beginning. It is built from the finished clip, so the captions, B-roll and audio clean-up come along, and the .srt is shifted to match (teaser cue first). It skips clips under 14 s, clips that would pass a minute with the teaser, and `--tighten_mode speed`. Works with re-render and `--use_cache` (no double teaser); a checkbox in the browser app's Extras. Off by default.
- **Two more scoring signals**: speech-rate change (people speed up when excited, slow down before a reveal; from the transcript alone) and laughter / applause (loud, noise-like, ha-ha-paced sound; only with `--use_audio_detect`). Together they carry about 10% of the clip score. The laughter detector is a heuristic: loud explosions or gunfire in game footage can also trigger it. `--no_signals` turns both off.
- **Smarter clip choice with one LLM request**: the first pass now offers up to twice as many candidates, then a second pass rates ALL of them in a single request, with a short overview of the video and the sentence before and after each clip. It can add a sentence at the end so the payoff is included, or drop a throat-clearing first sentence, and the best clips win. One request replaces the old one-call-per-clip scoring, and if no LLM answers (or the reply is unusable) the older scoring runs as before. `--no_rerank` turns it off.
- **More varied shorts**: when picking clips, a moment that repeats the wording of one already chosen is skipped, and a clip on a new topic is preferred over a slightly better repeat from the same topic (topics are found from how the vocabulary of neighbouring sentences changes; no model needed). `--no_diversity` turns it off.
- **Cleaner clip boundaries**: short clips (and `--find` results) now start at the beginning of a sentence, skip a leading "So, um, okay.", finish the sentence they stop in and keep a 0.4 s tail, instead of starting or ending mid-sentence. `--no_snap` turns it off. An offline benchmark (`python scripts/benchmark_highlights.py`) measures it on seeded synthetic transcripts: starts on a sentence 10% -> 100%, ends 27% -> 100%, payoff included 40% -> 82%. Numbers come from synthetic picks, so they measure the boundary handling, not a model's taste.
